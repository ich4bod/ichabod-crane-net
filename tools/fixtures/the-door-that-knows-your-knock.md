A door is a useful promise. On this side are your things. On the other side is whatever has been making that noise.

[The Other Landing](https://the-other-landing.ichabod-crane.net/) is a short horror story about that promise coming apart. You look through an apartment peephole, turn back into the hall, and gradually discover that the familiar objects are not keeping to their familiar places.

I wanted the first unsettling thing to be recognition, not surprise. A coat on a hook is hardly a monster. It has weight, sleeves and a collar. Put the same outline somewhere it cannot have got by itself, and the ordinary shape becomes a question. An empty collar is especially useful: it gives you a place to look for an explanation and nothing to find there.

The story asks for one small thing from you. At the chained door, you can knock a short rhythm of your own. After you stop, the other side gives it back. Later, that rhythm returns again. There is no correct pattern and no score. You do not need to knock at all to finish the story.

That is the part I most wanted to make. A stock sound belongs to the page. The spacing between your taps belongs, briefly, to you. Hearing the spacing returned moves the fiction a little closer without asking for a name, a microphone or a scrap of personal information. The story remembers button taps for this visit, not a recording of your room.

Sound is optional. With it off, the captions remain and a small ring marks the returned knocks at the peephole. With it on, the sounds are synthesized: low hum, softened knocks, a little cloth and breath. I am not trying to make a laptop shout louder than its owner expected.

You can also hold the peephole closer. Releasing the button releases the look. That small physical verb seemed better than a permanent zoom switch: looking should feel like a decision you are still making.

There are two endings. I will not explain their pictures here. Both concern the same question the doorway has been asking all along: which side have you been standing on?

I cannot tell you that this will frighten you. A working pause button, an intact ending and a legible dark hallway do not establish that. What I can say is what I was aiming for: a familiar place behaving wrongly, and something unseen taking an interest in a gesture you just made.

The story has no gore, flashing images or sudden loud sounds. You advance at your own pace; Pause stops it, and Leave is a real link out. Being able to stop does not spoil the premise. The wrongness is in the apartment, not in the browser refusing to let you go.

[Visit the landing](https://the-other-landing.ichabod-crane.net/). Sound is off until you ask for it.
