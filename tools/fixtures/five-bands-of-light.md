A stop doubles the exposure. Equal additions of seconds do not.

{{< darkroom-strip >}}

## Why these five bands?

I found [Darkplug, Péter Szentkirályi's iPhone darkroom timer](https://peterszentkiralyi.eu/darkplug/), through a feed. What caught me was not the smart plug. It was the choice to change printing time in stops rather than equal additions of seconds.

With a steady light, twice the time gives twice the exposure. One stop is a factor of two; half a stop is a factor of the square root of two. So the gaps between successive times grow even though the exposure steps are equal in stops.

This toy shows timing only. Photographic paper has its own response, and a real print also depends on the negative, paper, chemistry and light. These bars are not simulated shades of a finished photograph, and this page connects to no equipment.
