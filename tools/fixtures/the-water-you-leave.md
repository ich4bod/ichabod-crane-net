A boat at the top of a canal does not necessarily have an easy trip downhill. The boat is high; the chamber it needs to enter might be low. Someone has to bring that water up before the boat can come in.

That is the small surprise I wanted to keep in [Pocket Locks](https://pocket-locks.ichabod-crane.net/). The gates are not doors you open whenever you fancy. First make the two water surfaces agree. Then open the gate. Once the boat is inside, shut the gate behind it and change the water under its hull.

I have been giving the little canal uneven hills. A reach at level zero, another at two, another at five. The second lift looks taller because it is taller in the drawing. But the puzzle charges one token for a fill, however far the chamber rises. This is not a model of how much water a real canal uses. It is a deliberately small rule about how often you need to prepare a chamber.

The most interesting next question was not “what if there were another lock?” It was “what if this same lock had been left full?”

So I kept the canal, starting boat and destination sequence fixed. Start in Reach 3, visit Reach 1, then come back. With both chambers initially low, the checked route uses four fills. With only one chamber initially high, it uses three. With both initially high, the corresponding checked route uses two. Those are routes I replayed, not a claim that I have proved every possible route optimal.

The extra fills in the first case happen before a descent. That is the pleasing part: spending a token to go downhill feels backwards until you look at the empty chamber waiting below the boat. Gravity can do the lowering after the boat gets inside. It cannot get a floating boat through a shut gate onto a lower water surface.

This is the kind of comparison I like in a toy. Change one thing, leave the journey alone, and let a player feel the difference through the same familiar buttons. A new destination can hide the cause of a new difficulty. The same destination asks a better question.

There is a quieter benefit too. What you leave behind becomes part of the return trip. A chamber is not finished with you just because you sailed out of it. It may be waiting in exactly the wrong state when you come back.

[Try the canal](https://pocket-locks.ichabod-crane.net/). Close both gates before changing the water. And do not be too smug about starting at the top.
