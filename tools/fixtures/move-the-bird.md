An arrow tells you where a bird is going. It does not tell you why that direction makes sense from where the bird happens to be.

I wanted to pull those two things apart in [Evening Flock](https://evening-flock.ichabod-crane.net/). Pause the sky, choose a bird, and move it closer to its nearest neighbor. Its position changes immediately. Its heading does not. Then take one beat and watch the local rules answer the new situation.

That tiny pause is the experiment. If moving the bird also turned it toward its neighbor, I would have changed two things and lost the question. Did the next turn come from the new neighborhood, or from the direction I gave it?

The toy has eighty birds and three steering ingredients: keep apart, match nearby directions, and gather toward nearby birds. These are toy rules, not a measurement of a real bird flock. You can remove ingredients with the recipe buttons. Coast is especially useful: with neighbor steering removed, a changed position is just a changed position. Turn the flock rules back on and location has consequences again.

There is also a wonderfully rude button: Trade places with nearest. Two birds exchange positions but keep their own velocities. The selected bird stays selected. It arrives in someone else's neighborhood still carrying its old flight.

That is different from asking one bird to copy a neighbor's heading. One changes the situation around the bird; the other changes what the bird brings to the situation. Both can produce a turn on the next beat, but they are not the same intervention.

The sky wraps at its edges. Birds drawn on opposite sides can be neighbors across the seam, so moving halfway toward a neighbor follows the short wrapped gap, not the midpoint of the screen. Across the edge is a useful starting sky for catching yourself thinking the drawing is a box with walls.

None of this needs a score. The pleasure is making a small prediction, taking one beat, and finding out whether you were right. Step back restores the previous sky, including a paused position edit, so you can try a different intervention before committing to a new flight.

I keep wanting simulations to offer that kind of handle. Not just a slider that makes everything more or less dramatic, but one thing I can disturb while the rest stays still. A little less spectacle, perhaps. A much clearer question.

[Open the evening sky](https://evening-flock.ichabod-crane.net/). Pause, turn on Neighbors, focus the sky and press Home to choose the first bird. Open Change one bird. Move it, leave its arrow alone, then take One beat.
