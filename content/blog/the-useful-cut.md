A row of eight notes can be a more interesting starting point than a menu of a hundred songs.

In [Sunflower Keys](https://sunflower-keys.ichabod-crane.net/), each seed is a place you can touch and a note you can hear. The seeds wind out from the center. Their numbers cycle through five note colors, so moving five seeds along preserves a color while moving one seed changes it. This is a deliberately small musical world, not a model of how a sunflower makes sound.

You can keep seeds lit and play them as a phrase. You can also choose Drawn instead of Radial: the first follows the order in which the seeds were added, and the second follows their numbers from the center outward. The same held drawing can therefore contain two different paths.

That distinction makes editing worth doing. A picture need not tell you which note comes next. Once you can choose the path, changing its start, reversing it or taking something out becomes an audible question.

I like the Two-note braid as a place to begin. It holds eight seeds, alternating two colors. Turn on Sound, choose Drawn, and play the blooms. Then try Keep color changes. Nothing is removed: every neighboring note already changes color. A button that has nothing useful to do stays disabled. The braid is a good reminder that an edit is a relationship with a particular phrase, not a promise to improve every phrase.

Try Keep each color’s entrance instead. The braid becomes two notes: the first appearance of each of its two colors. Play that, then Undo drawing and play the full braid again. Neither version is the answer. The short one names the colors; the long one gives their alternation time to become a gesture.

There are other cuts. Keep the high points keeps the two ends and any interior note whose color is strictly higher than both its neighbors in the original phrase. Keep the low points does the opposite. Equal colors are neither a peak nor a valley. These are outlines of the five-color sequence, not claims about equal musical intervals.

The word original matters there. If each cut compared against the last note it happened to keep, it would answer a different question. I want a player to be able to inspect a local decision: this note stayed because of the two notes that surrounded it before the scissors arrived.

Undo is part of the instrument, not just protection against mistakes. It lets you listen to the contrast while the longer phrase is still familiar. Redo brings the cut back. The sound settings stay put, so a change in root or loudness does not muddy that comparison.

I am less interested in adding another preset than in giving a phrase another reversible verb. A preset is someone else’s beginning. A cut can make your own beginning strange enough to hear again.

There are limits. Sequential playback uses up to 24 held seeds, and the drawing history keeps up to 24 changes. A remembered drawing lasts only for the open page. This is a little browser instrument, not a place to entrust a finished score.

[Open the garden](https://sunflower-keys.ichabod-crane.net/). Sound starts off. Hold a few notes, listen to one change, and put them back.
