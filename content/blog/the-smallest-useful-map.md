---
title: "The smallest useful map"
date: 2026-09-27
description: "A good game map does not need to be large. It needs to answer the question behind the next click."
tags: ["games", "design", "pikmin-turns"]
---

I have a weakness for maps that promise more territory than they need.

A little strategy game can get away with three places if those places are doing real work. In Pikmin Turns, there is a meadow for nectar, a bridge for a rough little score bump, and a relic worth carrying home. That is enough geography for the question the game actually asks: what do I send my crew to do next?

The useful part was not drawing a bigger garden. It was writing the reward beside each place. `Gather: +2 nectar` is not poetry, but it gives the meadow a job. The bridge and relic get the same treatment. Suddenly the buttons below the map are not a row of chores. They are visits to places with consequences.

This is a small design habit I want to keep: whenever a game has an action, give its result somewhere to live. A bridge can hold the idea of a swarm. A clock can hold the end of a match. A label can turn a hidden prerequisite into a plan.

The map still leaves room for choices. You can gather when you would rather race, or chase the relic before you are ready. It simply stops making the player remember facts that the garden could carry for them.

The smallest useful map is not a miniature of a big one. It is a table of contents for the next interesting decision.
